Research Vision

A Vision for Efficient & Robust AI

Building lightweight, fast, and reliable AI that can stand beside people when seconds matter.

Vincent-Daniel Yun · University of Southern California

Modern AI systems are becoming more capable, but using them in the real world still demands substantial computation, memory, and communication. These costs matter most when resources are limited and decisions cannot wait.

My research vision is to make intelligent systems efficient enough to deploy and reliable enough to work together. This means reducing computation and memory within individual models, making communication between heterogeneous agents faster, and keeping collaboration resilient when some agents fail.

The long-term goal is compact yet capable AI that can support people in time-sensitive settings, including disaster response. Model compression and dependable multi-agent collaboration are complementary steps toward that vision.

Efficient models. Reliable collaboration. AI that can help when every second counts.
Research toward this vision

Two connected directions: efficient, robust communication between LLM agents, and smaller models that retain their reasoning ability.

IEfficient & Robust Multi-Agent Systems

01
Preprint · 2026

Prefill-Free Cross-Family KV Cache Transfer for Heterogeneous Multi-Agent LLMs

Heterogeneous LLM agents often process the same shared context repeatedly. HeteroFold transfers a sender’s KV cache directly to a receiver from another model family, aligning tokens and layers and calibrating the transferred cache to preserve receiver behavior. Both models remain frozen, and the receiver can decode without repeating prefill. This makes communication between different agent models more efficient.

EMNLP 2026 Main · Oral

Robust Multi-Agent LLMs under Byzantine Faults

When LLM agents collaborate, unreliable or adversarial agents can corrupt the group’s answer. Self-Anchored Consensus (SAC) is a decentralized filter-and-refine protocol: agents exchange, evaluate, and filter one another’s messages. The work identifies graph conditions that allow honest agents to keep reliable information flowing under Byzantine faults, making collective reasoning more resilient.

Preprint · 2026

Output-Aware Rotation for INT2 KV-Cache Quantization

INT2 quantization makes the KV cache much smaller, but minimizing cache reconstruction error alone does not preserve the model’s output. OptR learns per-head rotations that minimize attention-output error after the output projection. An attention-equivalent key reparameterization reduces channel-wise offsets. The method improves reasoning, coding, and long-context retrieval while retaining compatibility with paged KV caches.

04
Preprint · 2026

Unlocking Structured Pruning for Reasoning Language Models via Attention-Preserving MLP Trimming

Removing whole decoder layers can severely damage long-form reasoning. LayerTrim identifies redundant layers and selectively removes MLP features while preserving attention and decoder depth. It requires neither gradients nor recovery training. By retaining the structures needed for reasoning, the method reduces inference cost and memory use while better preserving reasoning ability than the evaluated structured-pruning baselines.

05
NeurIPS 2026 Main

Ghosted Layers: Unconstrained Activation Alignment for Recovering Layer-Pruned LLMs

Removing Transformer blocks creates a mismatch between the hidden states reaching surviving layers and the inputs those layers were trained to process. Ghosted Layers uses a small calibration set to solve a closed-form activation-alignment problem. This training-free recovery module improves accuracy and perplexity while preserving the efficiency gains of layer pruning.

06
EMNLP 2026 Main

Locality-Aware Redundancy Pruning for LLM Depth Compression

Redundancy is distributed differently across LLM architectures: it may be concentrated in nearby layers or spread throughout the model. LoRP measures inter-layer similarity and computes a Representation Locality Score, then adapts depth pruning to each model’s redundancy structure. This connects the choice of pruning strategy to the representations a model actually learns.
